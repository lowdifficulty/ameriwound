# WoundCare SOAP Notes AI Generator

## Product Knowledge Dump and Development Specification

**Version:** 1.0
**Updated:** July 26, 2026
**Primary purpose:** Generate accurate, complete, audit-ready wound-care SOAP note drafts from structured clinical inputs, dictation, prior notes, wound photographs, product records, and procedure details.

---

# 1. Product Definition

The WoundCare SOAP Notes AI Generator is a clinical documentation application for physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, nurses, wound-care specialists, and authorized support staff.

The system should:

1. Collect structured information about the patient, encounter, and each wound.
2. Import relevant information from previous visits.
3. Accept clinician dictation or typed notes.
4. Generate a structured SOAP note.
5. Generate separate procedure notes when procedures were actually performed.
6. Identify missing, inconsistent, or clinically important documentation.
7. Track wound progress over time.
8. Prepare coding suggestions for clinician and billing-team review.
9. Maintain complete product traceability for grafts, skin substitutes, dressings, and other wound-care products.
10. Require clinician review and signature before anything becomes part of the legal medical record.

The system must never fabricate:

* Wound measurements.
* Diagnoses.
* Symptoms.
* Examination findings.
* Procedures.
* Products used.
* Tissue removed.
* Anesthesia.
* Infection status.
* Patient consent.
* Medical necessity.
* Product waste.
* Lot or serial numbers.
* CPT, HCPCS, ICD-10-CM, or modifier selections.
* Treatment response.
* Compliance with offloading, compression, glucose control, or other therapies.

CMS expects wound records to show the wound’s current status, objective dimensions, infection status, nonviable tissue, response to treatment, treatment goals, complicating factors, and changes in the plan when healing is inadequate.

# 2. Product Positioning

## Recommended positioning

> “AI-assisted wound-care documentation that turns structured clinical information and provider dictation into complete SOAP note drafts, procedure notes, progress tracking, and documentation alerts.”

## Do not position it as

* An autonomous diagnosis engine.
* A replacement for a wound-care clinician.
* An automatic coding or billing system.
* A tool that determines Medicare coverage.
* A tool that proves medical necessity.
* A tool that selects treatments without clinician review.
* A tool that diagnoses infection, ischemia, osteomyelitis, or malignancy from an image alone.

FDA’s current clinical decision-support guidance emphasizes that intended use and the nature of the recommendation affect whether software may be regulated as a device. The application should therefore expose its reasoning, source data, limitations, and required clinician review instead of presenting opaque medical conclusions.

# 3. Core Safety Rules

Every AI-generated note must display:

> **AI-generated draft. The treating clinician must verify all measurements, findings, diagnoses, procedures, products, medical-necessity statements, and coding before signing.**

The system must distinguish between four data classes:

### 3.1 Confirmed data

Information directly entered or confirmed by the clinician.

Example:

* Length: 3.2 cm
* Width: 2.1 cm
* Depth: 0.4 cm
* Exudate: moderate serosanguineous

### 3.2 Imported data

Information pulled from a previous note, EHR, device, laboratory result, medication list, or product inventory.

Imported information must show its source and date.

### 3.3 Calculated data

Values derived from confirmed measurements.

Examples:

* Surface area.
* Volume.
* Percentage area reduction.
* Percentage volume reduction.
* Days since treatment began.
* Application number during the episode.

### 3.4 AI-inferred or suggested data

Possible conclusions generated by AI.

Examples:

* Suggested wound etiology.
* Suggested diagnosis code.
* Possible documentation deficiency.
* Possible infection concern.
* Possible need for vascular reassessment.

AI-inferred information must never silently enter the signed note. It must remain visibly labeled as a suggestion until accepted by an authorized clinician.

Each wound must receive a persistent wound ID. Never identify wounds only as “wound one” from visit to visit.

Example:

## 8.3 Measurements

Capture:

* Length in centimeters.
* Width in centimeters.
* Depth in centimeters.
* Measurement method.
* Clock orientation.
* Whether the measurement is pre-procedure or post-procedure.
* Whether measurements include intact skin.
* Whether multiple disconnected areas are being measured.
* Whether the wound is irregular.
* Undermining locations and maximum depth.
* Tunneling locations and depths.
* Exposed structures.
* Photograph availability.
* Measurement confidence.

### Calculations

The system may calculate:

`Estimated surface area = length × width`

`Estimated volume = length × width × depth`

Display both as estimates because wounds are rarely perfect rectangles.

The system should retain:

* Baseline area.
* Baseline volume.
* Previous-visit area.
* Previous-visit volume.
* Current area.
* Current volume.
* Percentage area reduction.
* Percentage volume reduction.
* Change in depth.
* Days since baseline.
* Trend direction.

### Percentage area reduction

`((baseline area - current area) / baseline area) × 100`

### Percentage volume reduction

`((baseline volume - current volume) / baseline volume) × 100`

Never calculate a percentage when:

* Baseline measurement is zero.
* Required measurements are missing.
* Measurement methodology materially changed.
* Separate wounds were merged.
* A wound divided into multiple wounds.

In those situations, display “not comparable” and require explanation.

CMS expects objective wound dimensions and evidence of progress at each relevant visit.

# 12. Assessment Section

The Assessment should contain clinician-confirmed diagnoses and a clear interpretation of the wound’s status.

For every wound, include:

* Diagnosis.
* Etiology.
* Laterality and location.
* Severity, depth, or stage.
* Current status: improved, stable, stalled, deteriorated, healed, or reopened.
* Supporting objective changes.
* Relevant comorbid factors.
* Infection status.
* Ischemia concern.
* Treatment response.
* Barriers to healing.
* Medical necessity for continued skilled care.

The generator should avoid generic statements such as:

> “The wound is improving.”

Preferred:

> “The left medial ankle ulcer decreased from an estimated area of 8.40 cm² to 6.72 cm² since the previous visit, with increased granulation tissue and reduced drainage. The wound remains open and requires continued skilled assessment, compression management, and local wound care.”

When a wound is not improving, the system should prompt the clinician to reassess:

* Infection.
* Ischemia.
* Pressure.
* Offloading.
* Compression.
* Edema.
* Nutrition.
* Glucose control.
* Medication effects.
* Adherence.
* Foreign body.
* Osteomyelitis.
* Malignancy.
* Inflammatory or atypical etiology.
* Appropriateness of the current treatment.

CMS guidance states that inadequate improvement may require reassessment of infection, metabolic, nutritional, or vascular barriers and modification of the treatment plan.

The clinician must confirm:

* Wound treated.
* Medical indication.
* Consent.
* Time-out when applicable.
* Anesthesia.
* Instrument or method.
* Tissue present before procedure.
* Tissue actually removed.
* Deepest tissue level removed.
* Total surface area debrided.
* Pre-procedure dimensions.
* Post-procedure dimensions.
* Bleeding.
* Hemostasis.
* Pain and tolerance.
* Complications.
* Dressing applied.
* Post-procedure instructions.
* Follow-up.

CMS documentation for debridement expects the anatomical location, instruments, anesthesia when used, tissue removed, depth, area, wound characteristics, immediate care, and follow-up. The documented tissue removal and depth must support the service reported. Simple cleansing or removal of secretions is not itself debridement.

## Debridement procedure template

**Procedure:** [Clinician-selected procedure]

**Wound:** [Persistent wound ID and location]

**Indication:**
[Clinician-confirmed reason the procedure was medically necessary]

**Consent:**
[Confirmed consent details]

**Anesthesia:**
[Type, amount, or none]

**Pre-procedure findings:**
[Dimensions, tissue composition, drainage, undermining, exposed structures]

**Technique:**
[Instrument or method] was used to remove [confirmed tissue type] to the level of [confirmed deepest tissue level].

**Surface area treated:**
[Confirmed area] cm².

**Post-procedure findings:**
[Dimensions and wound-bed findings]

**Hemostasis:**
[Method]

**Tolerance and complications:**
[Patient response]

**Dressing and follow-up:**
[Instructions]

## Hard-stop validation

Do not generate a completed debridement procedure note when any of the following is missing:

* Tissue removed.
* Deepest tissue level.
* Instrument or method.
* Area treated.
* Wound identity.
* Indication.
* Clinician confirmation that debridement occurred.

The AI may create an incomplete draft with visible placeholders, but it must not fill them itself.

## Current Medicare configuration warning

The application must use effective-dated payer rules. CMS changed Medicare payment treatment for skin substitutes in 2026, including payment as incident-to supplies and an approximately $127.28 payment rate for the finalized 2026 policy. CMS also withdrew separate MAC skin-substitute LCDs that had been scheduled to take effect January 1, 2026. Existing coverage rules and contractor-specific policies therefore still require careful verification. Do not hard-code one universal Medicare rule into the documentation engine.

# 19. Photographs

Photographs should:

* Be linked to the patient, encounter, and wound ID.
* Include date and time.
* Identify whether they are pre-procedure or post-procedure.
* Preserve the original image.
* Preserve image metadata.
* Store a hash or integrity value.
* Avoid destructive editing.
* Permit annotations on a separate layer.
* Identify scale markers when used.
* Avoid using AI enhancement that could change clinical appearance.
* Record consent according to practice policy.
* Never be the sole source for wound depth, infection, exposed structures, or tissue percentages.

CMS recognizes photographs as supporting documentation and recommends them in certain prolonged or repetitive debridement situations.

## Stalled-wound alert

Trigger an alert when configured criteria are met, such as:

* No meaningful reduction over a defined period.
* Increasing area or depth.
* Increasing necrosis.
* Increasing drainage.
* Recurrent infection.
* Repeated missed visits.
* Poor offloading adherence.
* Poor compression adherence.
* Missing vascular assessment.
* Missing nutrition assessment.
* Unresolved osteomyelitis concern.

The alert should say:

> “The recorded trend may indicate inadequate progress. Reassess the wound, etiology, perfusion, infection, pressure, adherence, nutrition, and treatment plan.”

It should not say:

> “The treatment has failed.”

unless the clinician makes and confirms that determination.

# 21. Coding Support

Coding must be a separate review layer.

## The AI can

* Suggest possible diagnosis-code families.
* Suggest possible procedure-code families.
* Explain which documented facts support a suggestion.
* Identify missing facts required to choose among codes.
* Check laterality.
* Check anatomical consistency.
* Compare documented procedure depth with selected code.
* Compare area with procedure-code thresholds.
* Compare product units with inventory records.
* Flag possible duplicate or bundled services.
* Display the applicable rule source and effective date.

## The AI cannot

* Finalize codes without human review.
* Infer a higher debridement depth than documented.
* Select a code because it pays more.
* Invent medical necessity.
* convert a dressing change into debridement.
* Report a procedure not documented.
* assume a separately identifiable E/M service.
* assume modifier 25 applies.
* assume a payer covers a product.
* assume authorization guarantees payment.

CMS states that the diagnosis codes must be supported by specific clinical information and that the procedure or product code must describe what was actually performed or supplied.

## CPT licensing

CPT content is copyrighted by the American Medical Association. Do not distribute an unlicensed CPT database or reproduce proprietary descriptors beyond what the organization is authorized to use. Build the system so licensed code content can be connected through an approved vendor.

# 22. Documentation Completeness Engine

Before allowing signature, run validation rules.

## Critical errors

Block signature when:

* Patient identity is missing.
* Date of service is missing.
* Rendering clinician is missing.
* An active wound lacks a location.
* Required measurements are missing without an explanation.
* A procedure is documented without confirmation that it occurred.
* Debridement depth is missing.
* Debridement tissue removed is missing.
* Product lot information is required but missing.
* Product used and discarded quantities do not reconcile.
* A procedure note identifies the wrong wound.
* The note contains unresolved contradictory laterality.
* A medication allergy conflict is present.
* A clinician has not reviewed AI-generated text.

## Warnings

Permit signature after clinician acknowledgment when:

* Photograph is absent.
* Wound-bed percentages do not total 100%.
* Pain was not assessed.
* Vascular status is missing for a lower-extremity wound.
* Offloading is missing for a plantar diabetic ulcer.
* Compression documentation is missing for a venous ulcer.
* Tobacco status is missing.
* Nutrition assessment is missing.
* The wound has not improved.
* Infection findings are incomplete.
* Follow-up interval is missing.
* Treatment goals are missing.
* A product was switched without a documented reason.
* An application is being continued without documented response.
* Pre- and post-procedure measurements are identical and may require confirmation.

# 23. Contradiction Detection

The system should detect conflicts such as:

* “No drainage” and “large purulent drainage.”
* “No exposed structure” and “bone exposed.”
* “Wound healed” and nonzero dimensions.
* “No procedure performed” and completed debridement note.
* Right foot in one section and left foot in another.
* 100% eschar and 80% granulation.
* No anesthesia and a recorded anesthetic dose.
* No product waste and a discarded quantity greater than zero.
* Wound improved despite an unexplained increase in area and depth.
* Pressure injury identified without a stage or explanation.
* Compression ordered despite an unresolved perfusion warning.
* Graft amount used greater than the package amount opened.
* Product expiration date before the application date.
* Post-procedure depth less than pre-procedure depth following excisional debridement without explanation.

The application should ask the clinician to resolve the contradiction. It should never silently choose one statement.

# 24. AI Generation Workflow

## Step 1: Gather data

Collect structured fields, dictation, imported records, prior notes, photographs, laboratory results, and product records.

## Step 2: Normalize

Convert:

* Units to centimeters.
* Common anatomical terms to standardized values.
* Dates to ISO format internally.
* Product names to catalog IDs.
* Wound references to persistent wound IDs.

Do not alter the clinical meaning.

## Step 3: Identify missing information

Ask targeted questions, such as:

* “Which tissue was removed?”
* “What was the deepest tissue level debrided?”
* “How much product was discarded?”
* “Was odor present after cleansing?”
* “Which wound received the product?”
* “Was the measurement taken before or after debridement?”

## Step 4: Generate the draft

Generate:

* Subjective.
* Objective.
* Assessment.
* Plan.
* Procedure note.
* Patient instructions.
* Coding-support summary.
* Documentation warnings.

## Step 5: Compare with source data

Every factual sentence should be traceable to:

* A structured field.
* Dictation.
* Imported clinical data.
* A calculation.
* A clinician-approved suggestion.

## Step 6: Clinician review

Show additions and transformations.

The clinician can:

* Accept.
* Edit.
* Reject.
* Mark as incorrect.
* Return to source data.

## Step 7: Sign and lock

The final note becomes immutable except through a formal addendum or amendment.

# 25. Recommended AI System Prompt

You are a wound-care clinical documentation assistant.

Your job is to transform clinician-provided information into a clear, complete, internally consistent SOAP note draft. You do not independently diagnose, prescribe, determine coverage, establish medical necessity, or finalize billing codes.

Rules:

1. Use only information supplied in the current encounter, confirmed historical records, or explicitly labeled calculations.
2. Never invent measurements, symptoms, examination findings, procedures, diagnoses, consent, product details, medical necessity, or treatment response.
3. Preserve uncertainty. Use “not assessed,” “not provided,” or a visible placeholder when information is missing.
4. Distinguish patient-reported information from clinician-observed findings.
5. Organize every wound under its persistent wound ID.
6. Preserve laterality and exact anatomical location.
7. Do not describe a procedure unless the clinician confirmed it was performed.
8. For debridement, require the instrument or method, tissue removed, deepest tissue level, area treated, anesthesia, tolerance, and post-procedure care.
9. For product applications, require the product name, package amount, amount used, amount discarded, reason for waste, lot number, expiration date, application number, wound treated, and dressing.
10. Do not infer infection from an image.
11. Do not state that infection is absent unless the available findings support the clinician’s assessment.
12. Do not infer wound depth from a photograph.
13. Do not convert cleansing into debridement.
14. Do not increase the documented severity or procedure depth.
15. When information conflicts, do not resolve the conflict yourself. List the conflicting statements and request clinician review.
16. Suggested codes must remain separate from the clinical note and require human confirmation.
17. Use concise clinical language.
18. Avoid repetitive boilerplate.
19. Include objective evidence when describing improvement or deterioration.
20. Mark the output “AI-generated draft requiring clinician verification.”

Output:

* Documentation status.
* Missing critical information.
* SOAP note draft.
* Procedure note draft, when applicable.
* Patient instructions.
* Coding-support suggestions.
* Consistency warnings.
* Source-data map.

# 31. Example SOAP Note

**AI-generated draft requiring clinician verification**

## Subjective

The patient presents for follow-up of the left medial ankle wound. The patient reports that drainage has decreased since the previous visit and denies fever, chills, or increasing pain. The patient reports using the prescribed compression wrap and elevating the leg most days. No new antibiotic use was reported.

## Objective

The patient is alert and in no acute distress. Edema of the left lower extremity is present.

### Wound `WOUND-L-MEDIAL-ANKLE-001`

The wound is located at the left medial ankle and is documented as a venous leg ulcer. Pre-procedure measurements are 3.2 cm in length, 2.1 cm in width, and 0.4 cm in depth, with an estimated surface area of 6.72 cm² and estimated volume of 2.69 cm³. The wound bed contains approximately 70% granulation tissue and 30% adherent slough. Moderate serosanguineous drainage is present. No odor is present after cleansing. The wound edges are irregular and attached. The periwound demonstrates mild maceration, edema, and hemosiderin staining. No tunneling or undermining is documented. No purulence, fluctuance, or spreading erythema is documented.

## Assessment

Chronic venous ulcer of the left medial ankle with exposed subcutaneous tissue, improving based on reduction in estimated area, increased granulation tissue, and decreased drainage. The wound remains open and requires continued skilled wound assessment, edema control, compression management, and local wound care. Venous insufficiency and lower-extremity edema remain contributing factors. No clinical evidence of acute infection was documented during this encounter.

## Plan

Cleanse the wound with the clinician-selected cleanser. Apply the selected primary dressing and secondary absorbent dressing. Protect the periwound with barrier film. Continue the prescribed compression system after confirmation of appropriate vascular status. Encourage leg elevation and adherence to compression. Review precautions for increasing pain, spreading redness, fever, rapidly increasing drainage, malodor, or other signs of deterioration. Return in one week or earlier for worsening symptoms.

# 36. Final Product Principle

The application should make accurate documentation easier without making inaccurate documentation easier.

The AI should help clinicians remember, organize, calculate, compare, and communicate. It should never silently create the clinical facts needed to justify a treatment, procedure, product, diagnosis, or claim.
